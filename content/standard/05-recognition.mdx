This specification standardizes how a community records **external recognition** — the acknowledgment it has received from outside actors — as a spectrum of standing rather than a single claim. Its purpose is to prevent the most common inflation in the field: treating any acknowledgment as recognition of statehood.

**Status:** Working specification v1 — the recognition doctrine is published canon; which forms of recognition should count toward network-state standing remains an open question, not a defined threshold.

The full doctrine is published at [What Is a Network State?](/frontier/network-state) and [What Is Sovereignty?](/frontier/sovereignty); the operational treatment is in the handbook at [External Relationships](/handbook/external-relations).

## 1. The definition

> **Recognition is when an outside actor acknowledges that an entity possesses a particular status.**

Recognition may come from individuals, companies, municipalities, courts, regional governments, sovereign states, or international organizations. **These forms of recognition are not equivalent.**

## 2. The non-equivalence rule

The central discipline of this specification: different acknowledgments carry different weight, and none may be reported as more than it is.

> A mayor acknowledging a community is not the same as a sovereign state recognizing another state. A partnership is not recognition of statehood. A protocol listing is not political recognition.

An introduction to a mayor, an MOU with a ministry, municipal cooperation, recognition by a sovereign government, formal diplomatic relations, and membership in an international organization **do not carry equal weight**. Recognition is a spectrum of external standing, not one vague claim.

## 3. The proof discipline

Recognition claims are subject to the same proof discipline as every other claim in the guide.

> **Diplomatic language does not prove diplomatic recognition.**

> **A meeting with government officials does not prove diplomatic recognition.**

Language, meetings, and aspiration are not standing. Only the actual acknowledgment, from the actual actor, at the actual level, is.

## 4. Recording rules

A community conforming to this specification records each acknowledgment as two facts and no more: **the specific actor** and **the specific status that actor acknowledged**. It does not aggregate distinct acknowledgments into a single headline claim, and it does not describe an acknowledgment at a higher level than the actor granted.

Recognition is also reported alongside capacity, never as a substitute for it:

> **Recognition does not create capacity, and capacity does not automatically create recognition.**

A community may be widely acknowledged and unable to perform, or highly capable and unacknowledged. The two are recorded separately.

## 5. Conformance

A recognition record conforms when it:

1. Names each acknowledgment by its specific actor and specific status.
2. Never reports an acknowledgment as more than the actor granted — no MOU as statehood, no partnership as political recognition, no protocol listing as sovereign standing.
3. Treats recognition as a spectrum of external standing, reported per-actor, never as one aggregate claim.
4. Keeps recognition and capacity as separate records.
5. Applies the proof discipline — language and meetings are not recorded as recognition.

## 6. What this specification does not define

Which forms of external recognition should count toward network-state standing is deliberately left open — the published reading order marks it as an open question, not settled doctrine. This specification standardizes how recognition is *recorded and reported honestly*; it does not rank the forms or set a threshold at which accumulated recognition becomes political standing.
